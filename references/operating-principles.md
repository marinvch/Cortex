# Operating Principles

How the Cortex Vault decides what to capture, what to automate, and what to build. These are
*our* working principles — synthesized from common knowledge-management and automation practice,
written for this vault. Three moves, in order: **Notice → Decide → Build.**

> Boring is beautiful. The best system is the one you barely notice running.

---

## 1. Notice — get it out of your head

The core habit: **knowledge leaves your head and lives in the vault.** Stop trying to remember;
trust retrieval. Two reflexes:

- **Capture reflex.** A thought worth having twice is worth writing once. Default to `/capture`
  or a line in today's daily note. Friction kills capture — keep it one step.
- **Leverage reflex.** Before doing any recurring task the manual way, ask: *"to what extent
  could AI or a saved note do this for me?"* It's never all-or-nothing. Maybe 80%, maybe 10% —
  you don't know until you ask.

If a task itches because you've done it three times, that's the signal. Surface it at `/level-up`.

## 2. Decide — turn "I should automate something" into "here's exactly what"

Five quick filters before building anything:

1. **Find the constraint.** What actually breaks or bottlenecks if your workload doubled?
   Fix the clog, not the shiny thing.
2. **Eliminate → Automate → Delegate, in that order.** First ask *"what if I just stop doing
   this?"* Don't automate waste. Only then automate; only then hand off to a person.
3. **Map the steps.** Trigger → inputs → transformations → decision points → destination.
   If you can't explain it to a person, you can't explain it to an AI.
4. **Pick the lowest autonomy that works.** Suggest < draft < supervise < auto. Default to the
   *lowest* level that solves it. Workflows beat agents. Earn more autonomy with proof.
5. **Tie it to an outcome.** More output, less time, fewer errors, or learning. "Because it's
   cool" is not a reason. If it doesn't move a number or teach you something, drop it.

## 3. Build — ship the boring version

- **Smallest steps.** One input, one output per block. Deterministic pieces first; add AI only
  where judgment is actually needed.
- **Validate each step before chaining.** Never build the whole pipeline then test end-to-end.
- **Ship the rough version, then iterate.** Real usage teaches more than planning. Perfectionism
  is the enemy of a working brain.
- **Run it manually first.** Watch it for a week before trusting it unattended.
- **Kill switch.** If something needs constant patching or earns less than it costs, tear it
  down. Knowing when to delete is as important as knowing when to build.

## The three felt signals it's working

You'll know the vault is real when:

1. **Knowledge leaves your head** — you stop rehearsing what you decided last month; you trust
   the vault to hold it.
2. **The brain becomes your first move** — when something new lands, you ask the vault before
   opening six tabs.
3. **It answers better than you would** — a question comes in and the vault answers faster, with
   exact sources, than you could from memory.
